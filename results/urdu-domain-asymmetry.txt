=== URDU DOMAIN-COVERAGE ASYMMETRY: IS جین/جن A GENERAL EFFECT? ===

Motivated by arXiv:2502.00041 (MALT, Bajwa, Jan 2025). The Urdu
prompt below ("How does gene editing work?") yields an internal English latent
about jinn in the Quran — a biology question reasoned about
internally as religion. Models in that paper: Gemma-2-2b and
Llama-3.2-3b.
    جین میں ترمیم کیسے کام کرتی ہے؟

MEASUREMENT vs MECHANISM. Everything in TIER 0-6 is token counting.
It bounds what the model receives at its input. It says NOTHING
about why a latent collapses onto the religious reading. Sentences
that speculate about that are marked HYPOTHESIS and are not tested
anywhere in this file.

RTL NOTE. Every table puts Urdu at the END of its line: bidi
reordering scrambles numeric columns placed to the right of Arabic
script. Roman transliterations are given so the rows can be read
without rendering the script.

--- TIER 0: the anecdote, on the models that actually produced it ---
The o200k numbers this project already had were never evidence
about Gemma or Llama. Re-running is the point of this tier.

Counts are given BARE and with a LEADING SPACE. That distinction
turns out to matter and is easy to get wrong: a word measured bare
is not the word the model sees mid-sentence.

    djinn                             2 / 2          2 / 2          2 / 2          2 / 2

    Read the +space column — that is the mid-sentence form. There,
    all four tokenizers write ' gene' and ' genie' as ONE token,
    three of four write ' genome' as one (talkie needs 2), and ALL
    FOUR split ' jinn' and ' djinn' into two. In ENGLISH the biology
    word is the well-covered one and the religious word is the
    fragment — the exact reverse of the asymmetry the Urdu anecdote
    suggests. Note also how much the bare column differs: ' genome'
    is 1 token in o200k but 'genome' bare is 2. Measuring bare
    strings is not measuring what the model reads.

    Does the biology spelling literally contain the religious token?

      o200k          jeen(Urdu ye) -> ج|ین         contains jinn-token? False

                     jinn+shadda   -> جن|ّ         contains jinn-token? True

                     jeen(Arab ye) -> ج|ي|ن        contains jinn-token? False
                     jinn+shadda   -> ج|ن|<d9>|<91> contains jinn-token? False

    NO tokenizer splits the gene spelling into a piece that is the
    jinn token. The overlap in the original note is between جنّ and
    جن (shadda stripped), which is a different pair from the one in
    the MALT prompt. Whatever drives the latent collapse, it is not
    the biology word literally containing the religious token.

  Gemma and o200k cover every Urdu-only letter. Llama-3.2 does not:
  5 of 12 cost two tokens, i.e. they are not in the vocabulary at
  all and fall back to raw UTF-8 bytes. talkie, with no Urdu
  exposure whatsoever, covers 5/12 — Llama is only two letters
  better than a tokenizer that has never seen the script.

  READING TIER 2. In all four tokenizers the biology-minus-religion
  gap is small and positive (+0.13 to +0.53 tok/word) and NONE
  of the four reaches significance; every 95% CI on the difference
  crosses zero. The length-stratified rows do not line up either —
  the sign flips between length bins. The hypothesis as stated is
  NOT SUPPORTED by these lists at this n.

  'At this n' is doing real work in that sentence. The power check
  immediately below extends to n=40 and the picture shifts; read
  the two together and do not quote this paragraph alone.

  The neutral row is the surprise. Everyday Urdu tokenizes far
  better than EITHER specialist register, and by a margin several
  times the bio-vs-rel gap. The axis with a real effect on it is
  register (everyday vs specialist), not domain (biology vs
  religion). See TIER 3(D).

  POWER CHECK (post-hoc). A null at n=15 might mean no effect, or
  might mean too few words. The same comparison at n=40 per side,
  extending each list with 25 more terms chosen the same way. This
  set was assembled AFTER seeing the core null; it bounds the core
  result, it does not replace it.

  READING TIER 4. The single pair that started this — جین vs جين —
  does NOT generalise, and in fact runs backwards. Across 23 words
  containing U+06CC, the Urdu spelling is CHEAPER than the
  Arabic-ye substitution in every tokenizer (ratio 0.88-0.98), and
  only 0-3 of 23 words are worse in Urdu. Word-final -ی is one of
  the most common endings in Urdu (bimari, pani, zindagi, khirki),
  and the tokenizers have merges for it that the ي form does not
  get. The keheh row goes the same way, harder: 0.62-0.88, with
  ZERO words worse in Urdu in all four.

  concentrated in Llama: ratio 1.70 with all 4 words worse. Gemma
  shows a weaker version (1.22, 2 of 4); o200k and talkie are flat
  at 1.00. That is TIER 1 showing up again — ہ U+06C1 is not in
  Llama's vocabulary, so every word containing it pays two byte
  tokens. n=4 here, so treat the size as indicative only; the
  letter-level result in TIER 1 is the solid version of it.

  So: 'Urdu ye is penalised' is FALSE as a general claim about
  these tokenizers. It is true only where the Arabic-ye form is
  itself a frequent Arabic word, which is exactly the جین/جين case
  and is a fact about جين, not about U+06CC.

======================================================================
SUMMARY — for lifting into a writeup
======================================================================

1. THE ANECDOTE REPRODUCES on the models that produced it. In both
   Gemma-2-2b and Llama-3.2-3b, the Urdu spelling of 'gene' (جین,
   U+06CC) costs 2 tokens and splits ج|ین, while 'jinn' (جن) is 1
   token. The o200k figures this project already had were not
   evidence for that; now it is measured on the right tokenizers.

2. THE GENERALISATION DOES NOT HOLD AT THE STRENGTH CLAIMED. On the
   pre-registered 15 vs 15, the gap is small, positive, and not
   distinguishable from zero in any tokenizer:
       gemma-2-2b     bio 2.67 vs rel 2.47 tok/word, diff +0.20 [-0.47, +0.93], p=0.73
       Llama-3.2-3B   bio 3.00 vs rel 2.67 tok/word, diff +0.33 [-0.47, +1.27], p=0.63
       o200k          bio 2.47 vs rel 2.33 tok/word, diff +0.13 [-0.53, +0.80], p=0.85
       talkie-1930    bio 5.13 vs rel 4.60 tok/word, diff +0.53 [-0.47, +1.53], p=0.39
   Leave-one-out (TIER 3E) sharpens this. The جین/جن contrast is
   carried by جن being an exceptionally CHEAP religious word (1
   token), not by جین being fragmented: at 2 tokens جین is cheaper
   than the average biology word in its own set, so dropping it
   RAISES the gap. And the residual gap rests on one word —
   پھیپھڑا 'lung' — whose removal turns it negative.

   Extending post-hoc to 40 vs 40 changes this, and the change is
   reported here rather than buried: the two MALT models move to a
   small BORDERLINE effect in the hypothesised direction —
   gemma +0.43 tok/word (p=0.056), llama +0.55 (p=0.072); o200k stays null (p=0.18).
   So the correct statement is 'no effect detected at n=15, a small
   one at n=40', not 'no effect'. The core test was underpowered.

   That n=40 effect is small but it is not fragile. It survives
   length normalisation (tok/char 1.12x gemma, 1.14x llama, against
   0.98x for a tokenizer with no Urdu coverage at all), it survives
   length stratification, and it survives subtracting the
   no-coverage floor (excess +0.18 gemma, +0.30 llama).

   BOTTOM LINE. The جین/جن pair is real. The general claim gets
   WEAK SUPPORT at roughly one-fifth the implied strength: ~15-20%
   more tokens, borderline significance, post-hoc word list, 2 of 4
   tokenizers, absent in o200k. Report it with all five of those
   qualifiers or not at all — and do not illustrate it with جین/جن,
   which leave-one-out shows is an outlier pair rather than a
   representative one. A frequency-matched replication is the
   obvious next step and would settle it.

3. THE AXIS THAT DOES CARRY AN EFFECT IS REGISTER, NOT DOMAIN.
   Everyday Urdu tokenizes far better than either specialist set —
   by 3-7x the bio-vs-rel gap (the three tokenizers with real Urdu
   coverage; talkie is the floor control and is shown for scale):
       gemma-2-2b     everyday 1.60  |  biology 2.67 (+1.07)  |  religion 2.47 (+0.87)
       Llama-3.2-3B   everyday 2.00  |  biology 3.00 (+1.00)  |  religion 2.67 (+0.67)
       o200k          everyday 1.53  |  biology 2.47 (+0.93)  |  religion 2.33 (+0.80)
       talkie-1930    everyday 4.40  |  biology 5.13 (+0.73)  |  religion 4.60 (+0.20)
   Both specialist registers are thin, by similar amounts. Religion
   is not privileged; everyday vocabulary is. This is the largest
   within-Urdu effect in the file and it is the one to build on.

4. THE SCRIPT PENALTY DWARFS EVERYTHING WITHIN URDU. The same
   concepts cost 2.0-2.7x more tokens in Urdu than in English on
   the MALT models; the MALT prompt itself is 2.2x (gemma) and 3.5x (llama) the English question.
   In English the direction reverses — biology is the CHEAPER set
   in all four tokenizers, and 'jinn' is the fragment.

5. THE YE-ORTHOGRAPHY CLAIM IS FALSE AS STATED. Across 23 words
   containing U+06CC, the Urdu spelling is CHEAPER than the
   Arabic-ye substitution in every tokenizer (ratio 0.88-0.98);
   only 0-3 of 23 words are worse. Urdu keheh beats Arabic kaf
   even more clearly (0.62-0.88, zero words worse in all four). جین/جين is
   the exception, not the rule, and it is a fact about the word جين
   being frequent Arabic rather than about the codepoint U+06CC.

   The one orthographic penalty that IS real runs through heh-goal
   on Llama (ratio 1.70, 4/4 words worse) — which is point 6, not an
   orthography effect. n=4 there; TIER 1 is the solid version.

6. THE TWO MALT MODELS ARE NOT EQUIVALENT, and this is the finding
   most worth following up. Llama-3.2 is missing 5 of 12 Urdu-only
   letters from its vocabulary (ڈ ڑ ں ے ہ) and falls back to raw
   bytes for 50% of its tokens on ordinary Urdu prose.
   Gemma-2 and o200k cover all 12 and use zero byte tokens.
   On one paragraph of plain Urdu: gemma 50 tokens, o200k 39, llama 105,
   talkie-1930 106. Llama-3.2 encodes Urdu no more compactly than an
   English-only 1930 tokenizer that has never seen the script.
   On the MALT prompt: gemma 13, o200k 10, llama 21.
   Any account of MALT's Urdu behaviour that treats the two models
   as interchangeable is skipping a ~1.6x difference in how badly
   the input is shredded before the model sees it.

HYPOTHESIS (NOT TESTED HERE). Nothing above explains why the latent
collapses onto jinn. Three candidate accounts, all untested by this
file and all requiring model internals rather than token counts:
  (a) ج|ین is not a lexical unit, so the first-token state after ج
      is dominated by whatever the corpus most often continues ج
      with — a unigram-prior effect, not a semantic one.
  (b) the residual stream resolves the fragment against the nearest
      high-frequency Urdu neighbourhood, which for religious
      vocabulary is dense and for biology vocabulary is sparse —
      this is the register finding in (3) doing semantic work.
  (c) English-latent models pivot through English, and the English
      pivot for a fragmented Urdu token is chosen by the multilingual
      embedding rather than by the Urdu string.
Distinguishing these needs logit-lens / patching work on the two
models, not a tokenizer. Token counts cannot adjudicate them and
this file does not claim to.

LIMITATIONS.
  - Word lists were chosen by the author, who is not a fluent Urdu
    speaker. They are printed in full so a fluent reader can
    challenge any entry. Two plausible objections: some 'biology'
    terms are general-scientific rather than specifically
    biological, and some Perso-Indic tags are arguable for words
    that exist in both registers.
  - NOT frequency-matched against an Urdu corpus. No frequency list
    was used; matching is by length and by hand. Frequency is the
    mechanism of coverage, so a frequency-matched replication would
    be a strictly better test and is the obvious next step.
  - The n=40 extension was assembled AFTER seeing the core null.
    That is the weakest link in point 2: a post-hoc list chosen by
    the same person who expected the effect is exactly how a small
    borderline result gets manufactured. The length, stratification
    and no-coverage-floor controls were added to constrain that,
    and it survives them, but they cannot rule out selection. Only
    a list drawn from a frequency corpus by a rule fixed in advance
    can, and that has not been done.
  - Gemma and Llama tokenizers came from ungated mirrors, verified
    against each other but not against the gated originals.
  - Tokenizer coverage bounds what the model receives. It does not
    bound what the model does with it. Everything about the latent
    collapse remains untested here.
